# Trademark Policy

The Apache-2.0 licence on this repository covers the **code**. Section 6 of that licence is
explicit that it grants **no rights to trademarks** — the names and branding are handled here
instead.

This policy exists so that anyone can use, modify, and redistribute the software freely, while
users can still tell whether a given build came from us. Those two goals only conflict if the
names are used to blur the line.

## What this covers

The names **mythical** and **mythicalOS**, the mythical wordmark and logo marks, and the visual
identity used at mythicalos.ai. These are marks used and claimed by the project; none are
registered, so no ® is used or implied.

## What you may do, with no permission needed

- **Say what is true.** State that your software works with, integrates with, is built on, is
  compatible with, or competes with mythicalOS. Use the name to refer to the project — that is what
  names are for.
- **Redistribute official releases unmodified**, under their existing names.
- **Write about it.** Articles, talks, tutorials, courses, reviews, benchmarks, comparisons.
- **Fork the code.** Apache-2.0 guarantees this and nothing here takes it back.
- **Run it commercially, including as a paid hosted service.** The licence permits this
  deliberately — see the licence section of the README. Describing your service as *powered by
  mythicalOS* or *a hosted mythicalOS deployment* is accurate and fine.

## What needs written permission first

- **Naming.** Using the marks, or a confusingly similar variant, as the name of your product,
  service, company, app, domain, or social account.
- **Modified builds under our name.** Apache-2.0 lets you modify and redistribute; the mark is how
  a user tells your build from ours. Change the name if you change the software.
- **The logo as your own**, or any use that implies endorsement, affiliation, sponsorship, or that
  your offering is the official one.
- **Merchandise.**
- **Registration.** Registering these marks, a variant, or a confusingly similar domain, in any
  jurisdiction.

## The rule behind the rules

If a reasonable person could come away believing your thing *is* mythicalOS, *is made by* us, or
*is endorsed by* us when it is not, that is the line. Everything above is an application of that
one idea, and it is the test we will apply to anything not listed.

## Questions

Ask before you build a brand on it — open an issue on this repository. Permission is often
straightforward, and it is much cheaper to get it early than to rename later.
